02 · Positioning

One word, one line, one statement

The guide asks for a single unifying word that is true, thrills the customer and that competitors want but can't claim. Then a phrase of five to seven words. Then repetition, every day, on every channel.

The word
Looked after
True. 66% of written reviews say it in their own words.
Thrills the booker. Their fear is the day going wrong on their watch. This removes it.
Rejects competitors. A community hall has nobody on site. A hotel's first job is its bedroom guests.
The line (tagline)
Looked after at Maggie's.
Four words. It uses the name people actually say, so the nickname becomes an asset rather than a gap between "Maggie's" and "O'Neill". It replaces "Space Made Simple" as the lead line. See taglines for the alternatives.
Positioning statement (internal)

For the people who organise training and meetings for care providers, the NHS, councils and charities across Merseyside, the Maggie O'Neill Centre is the hosted venue where someone meets your group at the door and everything is ready before they arrive. Unlike a community hall, you don't set anything up yourself. Unlike a hotel, rooms are the whole job, not a sideline to the bedrooms. And every booking funds Maggie's Meals.

The business-card test
"Maggie's is a charity-run training venue in Huyton. Someone meets your group at the door, the room's ready and the tea's on. Free parking outside."
The category we name
A hosted venue. Customers don't have a word for the gap between a hall and a hotel. We give them one and own it: "hosted training rooms", "hosted meeting rooms", "your host for the day".

Who we're happy to lose
The guide's first step to good positioning is knowing which customers you'll turn away. For us: individual hot-deskers, one-hour bookings from people shopping on price, and coworking-app traffic (the July Werksy enquiry is an example). They take the most staff time for the least revenue, and they pull the brand towards "cheap". We don't market to them, and we stop listing on coworking apps.
05 · Why they buy

Seven facts, one feeling, one mission

The guide builds positioning from three blocks: 5 to 7 hard facts, the emotion, and the social mission. Every fact below traces to a review.

Emotional: the feeling
Relief, then pride.
Relief for the booker that nothing will go wrong. Pride when her people come back saying it was lovely. For delegates at heavy courses (adoption, fostering, safeguarding), calm. "Nervous to attend… soon became at ease."
Mission: the reason to feel good
Every booking funds Maggie's Meals.
Only 4% of reviews mention it, so it's never the headline. It closes the deal: social value for public-sector procurement, and a reason for a private booker to choose us over a hotel at a similar price.

08 · Charging more

Stop competing with halls. Price against hotels.

"Don't make yourself cheap" is the guide's first rule. Price comes up in 9% of reviews and nobody says they chose us because we were cheapest. What people pay for is a day that runs without them.
