## What the training is (research, Sept 2026)
- Its full name is "The Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training on Learning Disability and Autism". People also call it "Oliver's Training" or OMMT. It's the government's preferred and recommended training for health and social care staff.
- The Health and Care Act 2022 says every CQC-registered provider must train its staff in learning disability and autism, at a level suited to each role.
- There are two tiers, and each has two parts. Part 1 is e-learning. Part 2 is a 1-hour live online session (Tier 1) or a **full face-to-face day (Tier 2)**. Only Tier 2 needs a venue.
- Tier 2 is **co-delivered**: a facilitating trainer, an autistic co-trainer and a co-trainer with a learning disability. These co-trainers are called "experts by experience" or people with "lived experience".
- The course covers reasonable adjustments. Reasonable adjustments should be made in advance, and based on the person's needs rather than their diagnosis. The venue should show that too.
- Demand won't go away: all staff need it, including porters, receptionists and catering teams, and bank, agency staff and new starters must be included. CQC expects to see evidence of it.
- Providers must also support and pay their lived-experience co-trainers properly.

## Who we're writing for
1. **Approved training providers** (like Be Well Learning), who book the venue.
2. **NHS, ICB and care learning teams**, who commission the training or book it directly.
3. **Lead trainers and co-trainers**, who judge the room on the day. For many co-trainers, a calm and predictable venue makes the difference between a good day and a hard one.

## What providers ask for (from your notes)
- Space for up to 60, cabaret style, with room for a wheelchair to move between the tables.
- A quiet room next to the Auditorium, so anyone can step out, decompress and calm their senses.
- A place that meets the needs of trainers and delegates who are autistic, have a learning disability, or have access needs.
